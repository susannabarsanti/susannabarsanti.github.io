About

Hello! I'm Susi, currently a postdoctoral researcher at University College Dublin, primarily working with Dr Niels Warburton. I grew up in the Pisa countryside, in a small place called Monte Spazzavento, splitting my time between studying physics and helping (or, more honestly, trying my best to avoid) the olive harvest. After a bachelor's degree in physics — spent mostly in Aula Ponte at Polo Fibonacci — I moved to R(h)ome for my master's and PhD, living close enough to the Colosseum to make it my regular jogging route. Not bad at all! After graduating, I spent a brief (and rainy) spell in Nottingham as an Angelo Della Riccia Fellow, before eventually landing in Dublin — where I'm doing my best to make the most of Ireland's beautiful nature, Guinness, and pub music.

My PhD supervisors were Andrea Maselli, Leonardo Gualtieri and Paolo Pani, and it's during those years that I became fascinated by strong-field tests of General Relativity — especially the kind you can only probe with extreme mass-ratio systems like EMRIs.

Research Areas

Gravitational Physics

At the core of everything I do is gravity in its most extreme regimes — from the last orbits of merging black holes to the gravitational-wave signals that let us listen in on them.

Waveform Modelling

Building accurate gravitational-waveform models for Extreme Mass-Ratio Inspirals.

Fundamental Physics

Alternative theories of gravity and new physics beyond General Relativity and the Standard Model.

Data Analysis

Parameter estimation and forecasting via Fisher-matrix and Monte Carlo methods.
