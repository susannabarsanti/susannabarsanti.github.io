About

Hello! I'm Susi, currently a postdoctoral researcher at University College Dublin, primarily working with Dr Niels Warburton. I grew up in the Pisa countryside, in a small place called Monte Spazzavento, splitting my time between studying and helping with (or, more honestly, trying my best to avoid) the olive harvest. After a bachelor's degree in physics, I moved to R(h)ome for my master's and PhD, living close enough to the Colosseum to make it my regular jogging route – not too bad! There, supervised by Profs. Andrea Maselli, Leonardo Gualtieri and Paolo Pani, I became fascinated by black holes, gravitational waves, and exciting ways to use them to probe fundamental physics. After graduating, I spent a brief (and rainy) spell in Nottingham as an Angelo Della Riccia Fellow, before eventually landing in Dublin — where I'm doing my best to make the most of Ireland's beautiful nature and pub music.

Research Areas

Gravitational Physics

At the core of everything I do is gravity in its most extreme regimes — from the last orbits of merging black holes to the gravitational-wave signals that let us listen in on them.

Waveform Modelling

Building accurate gravitational-waveform models for Extreme Mass-Ratio Inspirals.

Fundamental Physics

Alternative theories of gravity and new physics beyond General Relativity and the Standard Model.

Data Analysis

Parameter estimation and forecasting via Fisher-matrix and Monte Carlo methods.

Projects

ModifiedEMRIWaveforms

Python package for the fast generation of gravitational waves from asymmetric binaries with scalar fields, building on FastEMRIWaveforms (FEW).
